Non-Visual Chemistry

Tools for reading spectra, molecular shape and conformational energy by ear and by hand, built for students who are blind or have low vision.

Each tool works two ways: with a PlayStation DualSense controller, which taps, vibrates and pushes back through its triggers, or with the keyboard and sound alone. No controller is required.

No controller connected. The keyboard and sound path works without one.

Modules

1. Spectra by ear and hand

Move along a ¹H NMR spectrum and hear each signal: pitch follows chemical shift in ppm, and the controller taps once for each line of a singlet to a quartet, with one distinct pattern for anything more complex. A spoken readout gives the shift, multiplicity, number of hydrogens and J in Hz, and a spoken summary lists every signal from high to low shift. IR follows, read in cm⁻¹.

Next. ¹H NMR first.

2. Energy by force

Feel the Barrier, made fully usable without a screen: the trigger pushes back with the energy of each conformation, and spoken readouts give the dihedral angle, energy in kJ/mol and conformation name.

Planned, after NMR.

3. Shape by touch

Probe around a central atom and feel one bump per bonded atom, so tetrahedral (109.5°), trigonal planar (120°) and linear (180°) geometries feel different.

Planned.

Without a controller

Every action has a key, every reading can be spoken, and taps play as clicks. Pages are tested for keyboard use and screen readers (NVDA on Windows, VoiceOver on macOS).

Browser support

Direct controller access, needed for trigger resistance and full-strength taps, works in Chrome and Edge on a computer. Other browsers can read the controller's sticks and buttons and give weaker vibration.
